Our story

A Chinese street kitchen, dropped onto JP Marg

Yang Dengke came to Nepal with a wok and a recipe list. Kathmandu already had plenty of Chinese restaurants — but most had been softened for the local palate. He wanted the real thing: numbing peppercorns, black vinegar, chilli oil, and noodles slapped against the counter until they were springy.

So he set up Kungfu Noodles to look like the street-food stalls back home. One wall is covered with photographs of every dish. Fifteen noodle bowls, twenty side dishes, a team of seven cooking and serving from morning until the lanterns come on.

Today most of the room is regulars. The rest found us on the way to Durbar Square and stayed for a second bowl.
